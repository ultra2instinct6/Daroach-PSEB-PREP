Metals Reacting with Water (ਧਾਤਾਂ ਦੀ ਪਾਣੀ ਨਾਲ ਪ੍ਰਤੀਕਿਰਿਆ)

Metals react differently based on their position in the reactivity series. The highly reactive metals react vigorously, while less reactive metals require heat or steam, and the least reactive do not react at all.

🚨 Exam Tip: Floating Metals

Calcium (Ca) and Magnesium (Mg) float when reacting with water. Why? Because the bubbles of hydrogen gas formed stick to the surface of the metal, making it buoyant!

Temp
2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g) + Heat
Sodium (Na) & Potassium (K): React violently. Exothermic reaction; hydrogen catches fire.

Calcium (Ca): Reacts less violently and floats due to hydrogen bubbles.
Mg(s) + 2H2O(l) → Mg(OH)2(aq) + H2(g)
Magnesium (Mg): Does not react with cold water. It reacts gently with hot water to form magnesium hydroxide and hydrogen gas. It also floats.
3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) → Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)
Al, Fe, Zn: They do not react with liquid water at all. They only react when exposed to gaseous steam to form metal oxides (not hydroxides).
Metal(s) + H2O(l/g) → No Reaction
Lead (Pb), Copper (Cu), Silver (Ag), Gold (Au): These metals are at the bottom of the reactivity series. They exhibit zero reaction with water under any condition.
